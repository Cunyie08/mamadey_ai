Find the high-risk pregnancy before it becomes an emergency.

Many pregnant women in Nigeria stop antenatal care early and give birth at home, where no clinic record sees them. MamaDey AI gives community health workers a quick risk check during a home visit, explains the result in plain words, and says when to refer.

How it works

AskThe health worker answers about fifteen questions a woman can answer at home: her age, past births, and the antenatal care she has had so far.
ScoreA machine learning model trained on Nigeria Demographic and Health Survey data estimates her risk and names the factors that raise it most.
ActHigh-risk women are flagged for referral to the nearest primary health centre, and every screening creates a digital record, even for a woman who never reaches a clinic.

What the testing shows

The model uses only information available during pregnancy. It was tested once on 673 held-out records from the South-West zone of the Nigeria DHS 2018, plus modelled records of women who leave antenatal care early.

The strongest warning signs were gaps in routine care: no urine test, blood pressure not checked, and antenatal care from a traditional birth attendant only. This is a research prototype, not a medical device; a pilot with community health workers in Ogun State is the next step, and its data will be used to retrain and recalibrate the model.

Built for safety and openness

Women are recorded by ID, not name, and only with consent. The prototype keeps data on the health worker's device. The code is open source so health ministries and partners can inspect, adapt and reuse it.
